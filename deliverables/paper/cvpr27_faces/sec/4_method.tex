\section{Cross-face constrained decoding}
\label{sec:method}

%TL;DR Idea: consistency is a property of voxels, so keep a voxel state while sampling faces and let it decide which tokens are legal.
Consistency is a statement about voxels, while the model writes cells. \cfd{} decoding bridges the
two with a state over the box that records what the faces written so far have committed, and it
masks the model's next-token distribution to the tokens that agree with that state.

%TL;DR The shell state holds known voxels (with their block) and empty voxels; each committed cell adds one known voxel and marks the voxels in front of it empty.
\paragraph{Shell state.}
The state holds two maps over the box: \emph{known}, which stores the block a face has placed on a
voxel, and \emph{empty}, which marks voxels some ray has passed through. When the decoder commits a
cell $(d,b)$ of face $f$, it sets voxel $r_f(c)[d]$ to known with block $b$ and marks $r_f(c)[0{:}d]$
empty. A see-through cell marks its whole ray empty.

%TL;DR The legal set for a later cell: depths not already seen through and not behind a known voxel; a known voxel forces its block; see-through only if no known voxel is on the ray.
\paragraph{Legal tokens.}
For a cell $c$ of a later face, walk its ray from the front. Let $d^\star$ be the depth of the first
known voxel on the ray, if there is one. A depth $d$ is legal if $d \le d^\star$ and voxel
$r_f(c)[d]$ is not empty, because a first hit cannot lie behind a known block or on a voxel another
ray has passed through. If the chosen depth lands on a known voxel, the block token is forced to that
voxel's block. See-through is legal only when no known voxel lies on the ray. When exactly one token is
legal, the decoder writes it without querying the model. Everything the earlier faces left open stays
the model's choice, including a block in front of a known voxel where no ray has passed.

%TL;DR Proposition: under this decoder every sampled face set is consistent; proof sketch by induction over committed cells.
\begin{proposition}
\label{prop:consistent}
Every face set sampled with \cfd{} decoding is consistent.
\end{proposition}
\emph{Proof sketch.} By induction over committed cells, the state is the lift of the partial face
set, and every committed cell equals the projection of that lift along its ray: its depth is the
first non-empty voxel on the ray, because no earlier voxel on the ray is known and every voxel the
cell marks empty stays empty, and its block is the block of that voxel. A later cell can only add
known voxels at legal depths, which never lie in front of an earlier first hit on any ray, so no
earlier cell changes under the lift. \TODO{write out the full proof in the supplement; check the case of

%TL;DR The constraint composes with any sampler that draws whole face sets, such as best-of-N with a reward, and with fine-tuned models.
\paragraph{Composition.}
\cfd{} decoding changes only which tokens are legal, so it composes with temperature, top-$k$, and any
selection method that draws whole face sets. We use best-of-$N$ selection with a structural reward,
\emph{face solidity}, the share of lifted blocks that touch another block, and also apply the decoder to a
model fine-tuned on its own best samples. \TODO{one-line definition of face\_solidity from the code.}

%TL;DR Cost: one state update per cell and a mask over depth tokens; about four extra minutes on 32 minutes of sampling.
\paragraph{Cost.}
The state update and mask take time linear in the box extent per cell and run on the CPU beside
batched GPU sampling. Sampling 399 face sets at batch 32 took 32 to 36 minutes with the constraint and
about four minutes less without it.
