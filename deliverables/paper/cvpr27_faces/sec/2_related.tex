\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}

%TL;DR Multi-view and 2.5D generation share our factorization, but in continuous space consistency is approximate and learned; ours is exact and enforced. They do not apply directly because they output RGB/depth for meshes, not discrete block grids.
\paragraph{Multi-view and 2.5D generation.}
MVDream~\citep{shi2024mvdream}, SyncDreamer~\citep{liu2024syncdreamer} and Wonder3D~\citep{long2024wonder3d}
generate several views of one object and encourage agreement through shared attention or synchronized
denoising. MVDD~\citep{wang2024mvdd}, Direct2.5~\citep{lu2024direct25} and recent 2.5D latent
models~\citep{latents25d2025} generate depth or normal maps and fuse them into a mesh or point cloud.
All of them share our factorization of a 3D object into images, and all of them reduce view
disagreement without removing it, because consistency between continuous images has no exact test. We
differ in two ways. Our faces are exact projections of a discrete grid, so we check consistency cell by
cell, and we enforce it at decoding time instead of learning it. These methods output RGB, depth or
normals for continuous shapes and do not produce block identities, so we do not run them as baselines;

%TL;DR Native 3D generators (voxel AR, octree AR, sparse-voxel diffusion, Minecraft-specific models) avoid view consistency but pay a cubic or hierarchical cost; we compare against a voxel AR on the same data.
\paragraph{Native 3D generation.}
TRELLIS~\citep{xiang2025trellis}, XCube~\citep{ren2024xcube} and OctGPT~\citep{wei2025octgpt} generate
structured 3D latents, sparse voxel hierarchies or octrees and never face the consistency problem,
at the cost of a representation that grows with volume or needs a learned hierarchy. For block worlds,
VoxelCNN~\citep{chen2019voxelcnn} places blocks one at a time, Dream-Cubed~\citep{merino2026dreamcubed}
trains masked diffusion on two million $32^3$ chunks, and BrickGPT~\citep{pun2025brickgpt} writes LEGO
bricks as text. Our voxel baseline is an autoregressive transformer~\citep{vaswani2017attention} over
the same houses and the same split as our face model, which isolates the representation from the data.

%TL;DR Constrained decoding enforces formal languages in LMs; we apply it to a geometric invariant whose legal set depends on all earlier faces, not on a grammar.
\paragraph{Constrained decoding.}
Language models generate valid SQL, JSON or code when a decoder masks tokens that a parser or grammar
rejects~\citep{scholak2021picard,geng2023grammar,willard2023outlines}. \cfd{} decoding uses the same
mechanism for a different kind of constraint. Its legal set is not given by a grammar over the token
string; it follows from a 3D state built by every earlier face, and it fixes about a fifth of all
cells outright. To our knowledge, no prior work enforces exact multi-view consistency by decoding.

%TL;DR Procedural and inverse-procedural facade modeling generates exteriors from rules; we learn faces from data and handle six faces jointly.
\paragraph{Procedural buildings and facades.}
Shape grammars generate buildings and facades from hand-written rules~\citep{muller2006procedural,muller2007facades},
and Infinigen generates whole scenes procedurally~\citep{raistrick2023infinigen}. In Minecraft, the
Settlement Generation Challenge evaluates rule-based builders by human
judgment~\citep{salge2018gdmc,salge2020gdmcreport}. These methods produce consistent geometry by design
but need rules per style. We learn faces from player builds and obtain consistency from the decoder
instead of the rules.
