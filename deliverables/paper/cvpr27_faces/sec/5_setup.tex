\section{Experimental setup}
\label{sec:setup}

%TL;DR Data: Minecraft houses at up to 32^3 with a pinned train/val/test split; 86% show a door from outside.
\paragraph{Data.}
We use 2{,}661 Minecraft houses at up to $32^3$ from GrabCraft and two public corpora~\citep{chen2019voxelcnn,comino2025text2mc},
deduplicated and split 70/15/15 by build group with a fixed seed. The face model trains on the faces
of the training houses, augmented with the eight symmetries of the square footprint (D4). We evaluate
on 399 test boxes, and on the first 256 of them where a comparison needs the protocol's paired card at
$n{=}256$. \TODO{report face-shard size (2,114 training houses in T68) and why it differs from 70\% of 2,661.}

%TL;DR Models: the 20M joint face transformer with D4 augmentation; a 5M variant and the independent-face model as ablations; a voxel transformer on the same houses as the 3D comparison.
\paragraph{Models.}
Our main model is a 20M-parameter \jointface{} transformer trained on the D4-augmented faces (selected
at step 18{,}000 of 24{,}000 by holdout likelihood). Ablations use a 5M \jointface{} and a 5M
\indepface{} model. The 3D comparison \voxelar{} is a 27M-parameter transformer over learned,
orientation-aware 3D block pieces trained on the same houses and split \TODO{name the arm of record
(canon\_oriented 5M or the 20M legacy model) and its card}. All models sample with top-$k$ 40.

%TL;DR Decoders: Free, a Grammar control that only enforces per-face syntax, and Cross-Face; each at T=1.0 and T=0.7; optional best-of-4 and an RFT model.
\paragraph{Decoders.}
\freedec{} samples the model as is. \grammar{} forces face headers and the end token and makes every
cell either see-through or an in-box depth followed by a block, with no state across faces; it is the
control that isolates the cross-face rule. \cfd{} adds the rule of \cref{sec:method}. We run each at
temperature 1.0 and 0.7, and the \grammar{} and \cfd{} decoders also under \bestof{4} selection and on
the fine-tuned model.

%TL;DR Baselines and references: Patchwork (six real faces from six houses), unigram and Markov face baselines, and the Real-Shell reference.
\paragraph{Baselines and references.}
\patchwork{} lifts six real faces drawn from six different training houses, so every face is real but
the set is not. Unigram and Markov baselines sample cells from per-face statistics of the training
faces. \shellref{} lifts each test house's own faces and is the target a perfect face generator reaches.

%TL;DR Metrics: consistency rate and reprojection error; parts per building; Geom-KID and MV-KID with Holm-corrected paired permutation tests; BlockScore from the concurrent protocol.
\paragraph{Metrics.}
\emph{Consistency} is the share of face sets with $\proj(\lift(F)) = F$, and \emph{reprojection error}
the share of cells that change. \emph{Parts} counts 26-connected components of the lift (real houses
2.7). \geomkid{} is a kernel distance on voxel geometry descriptors and \mvkid{} a kernel
distance~\citep{binkowski2018kid} on DINOv2~\citep{oquab2024dinov2} features of four rendered views.
\blockscore{}~\citep{anonymous2027blindness} reports the worst of five realism pillars in units of the
real-versus-real noise floor, and disqualifies generators that copy training builds or repeat
themselves. We compare arms with paired permutation tests (10{,}000 permutations) and correct for
multiple comparisons with Holm's method~\citep{holm1979simple}. \TODO{decide whether to score every arm
