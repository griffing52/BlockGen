\begin{abstract}
%TL;DR (X) We generate 3D block structures through their six faces, because faces cost far fewer tokens than voxels.
%TL;DR (Y) Faces generated one after another rarely describe one object; only 0 to 2% survive a lift to 3D.
%TL;DR (Z) Cross-face constrained decoding enforces exact agreement during sampling, without training.
%TL;DR (1a) It removes inconsistency, halves fragmentation, composes with best-of-N, and matches a voxel transformer on geometry while beating it on appearance.
%TL;DR (1b) Consistency holds by construction; a study from one face to six faces to voxels locates where 2D generation breaks.
Generating a 3D block structure voxel by voxel is expensive: a $32^3$ Minecraft house is up to
28{,}672 voxels, while its six axis-aligned depth-and-block faces are at most 5{,}632 cells, and the
gap widens with resolution. Faces generated one after another, however, rarely describe the same
object. Under free decoding, 0 to 2\% of the face sets a transformer produces survive a lift to 3D
unchanged. Block worlds make this agreement exact and checkable, so we enforce it. Cross-face
constrained decoding tracks what earlier faces have committed and restricts every later face to the
depths and blocks that agree with them. Every face set is then consistent by construction, without
retraining, and a fifth of all cells need no sampling. On Minecraft houses the constraint reduces
the number of disconnected parts per building from 33.7 to 12.9 and the multi-view render distance
from 0.299 to 0.166, and with best-of-4 selection to 0.098. With best-of-4 selection, the face generator
ties a voxel transformer on geometry and beats it on appearance.

to six faces to full voxels shows that agreement between views, not the realism of a single facade,
is where 2D generation breaks. \UNMEASURED{last sentence rests on the per-rung study, Sec.~\ref{sec:ladder}}
\end{abstract}
