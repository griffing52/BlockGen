# CVPR 2027 Review: "A Building Is Not Its Silhouette: Structural Blindness in 3D Generation Evaluation"

Reviewer profile: generative-model evaluation metrics and statistics (FID/KID bias, MMD estimators, kernel two-sample tests, PRDC, FLD, CMMD, metric meta-evaluation, multiple comparisons).

Material read: main.pdf (all 10 pages) and supp.pdf (all 24 pages). I looked at the anonymized code in `blockgen/eval/bench/` (`distances.py`, `ladder.py`, `compare.py`, `stats.py`, `geometry.py`) only to check that the paper describes what the code computes. Line numbers below are the paper's margin line numbers. "Supp." means the supplement.

---

## 1. Summary

The paper argues that render-and-embed metrics (multi-view DINOv2 KID and similar), surface-point metrics (Uni3D, ULIP-2, chamfer, EMD) and an exterior-view VLM judge cannot see the interiors of discrete 3D structures. The main probe is FILL-INTERIOR, which fills every sealed air cell of real Minecraft houses with the build's dominant material. Its converse, HOLLOW-SHELL, deletes every block that does not border exterior air. MV-KID places the filled corpus at +0.35 "noise floors" from real data. V-KID, an unbiased KID on a 77-d D4-invariant occupancy descriptor, places it at +444. Under an opaque renderer the invisibility is a short occlusion argument (Supp. H, Lemmas 1-3). The paper also proposes a gating protocol (ordering, invariance, self-consistency, n-stability, plus memorization, diversity and near-twin disqualification gates), a worst-pillar aggregate called BlockScore, a 40-generator rank-agreement study (Spearman 0.50, 76 reversed pairs), a LEGO replication including BrickGPT, and a 290-object Objaverse check.

## 2. Strengths

- **The controls are clean.** FILL-INTERIOR, HOLLOW-SHELL, MATERIAL-SHUFFLE, MONOCHROME and the mass-matched GROW-SHELL are well designed. GROW-SHELL in particular rules out "V-KID just counts blocks".
- **There is a positive control.** The same encoder and statistic recover the signal on cutaway views. This correctly locates the failure in the observation rather than the encoder.
- **The bit-identity checks are real.** Supp. H separates the physical glass leak from the atlas artifact and the GPU-raster nondeterminism. This is unusually careful.
- **The estimator choice is sound.** The paper uses the unbiased MMD estimator, explicitly rejects the biased estimator, warns that z-units depend on n (Supp. D.2), and, after an internal audit, uses an exact label-permutation test with Holm correction (Supp. D.4).
- **The robustness work is broad.** It covers random descriptor subsets, generic orbit-averaged random 3D CNNs (Supp. Tab. 15) and a threshold sweep over the gates (Supp. C.3, Fig. 5).
- **The contamination case study is a valuable negative result.** A required split declaration caught what no distribution statistic saw (Supp. L.2).
- **The squared-statistic reading caveat is useful.** The MMD² mixture identity and the √KID reporting recommendation (Supp. D.5) give practitioners something they can apply.

## 3. Weaknesses (ranked by severity)

### W1. The "noise floor" unit is a test statistic, not an effect size, yet the headline claims compare magnitudes across metrics, runs and n.

A "noise floor" is (v_arm − v_REAL)/sd_null. This is a null-standardized z, so it grows linearly in n for a fixed population effect (Supp. D.2: 132.9 to 286.7 when n goes from 64 to 128). It also depends on reference size (the same probe at n=256 reads 444.36 against 399 reference builds and 120.8 against 512, Supp. D.2 and Tab. 9), on the metric's effective dimension, and on whitening. The paper states the caveat but does not follow it:

- **Cross-metric ratios are used as findings.** Examples: "a factor of 1,270 between two pillars" (§6.4, l.502), "geometric gap 4.4 times its render gap" (abstract, l.029; BrickGPT 51.48/11.77), "only 1.2×" (l.511), and "a hundred times smaller than the 444 of V-KID" (Supp. G.2). The two metrics' floors scale differently with n: 0.72 per doubling for MV-KID versus 0.49 for V-KID (Supp. D.2). So these ratios move with n. At larger n the BrickGPT ratio grows, and at smaller n it shrinks. The 1,270 also divides by 0.35, which is a draw from noise; the paper itself says that row should be read as "indistinguishable, not as a number" (Supp. D.2, l.311).
- **Main Table 2 and Fig. 1c violate the paper's own D.2 rule.** They put n=256 leaderboard values (399-build reference), n=256 values against a 512-build reference (the cutaway rows, from Supp. Tab. 9), a separate converse-probe run (HOLLOW-SHELL V-KID +231.1), and n=128 point-cloud values against a 248-build reference in one column of "noise floors".
- **The same probe at the same n spans a factor of 2.6.** FILL-INTERIOR V-KID at n=128 reads 111.4 (Supp. Tab. 12 ladder), 201.8 (protocol run), and 286.7/287 (dose run, §6.1 and Supp. Tab. 8). D.2's twelve-seed range is 185 to 274. A unit that varies this much is not a scale on which to quote three significant figures (444.36, 422.43).
- **The finite-population correction may be wrong in the regime that matters.** The floor at n=256 is estimated from overlapping 256-of-399 draws, corrected by dividing the observed sd by √(1 − n/N) (`ladder._noise_floor`, Supp. D.3). That factor is correct for the non-degenerate (linear) part. Under the null, MMD² is close to a degenerate U-statistic. For a degenerate order-2 U-statistic under sampling without replacement, the leading-order variance factor is (1 − f)², not (1 − f). The sum of the 2-, 3- and 4-index covariance terms gives p₂ − 2p₃ + p₄ ≈ f²(1 − f)². The released code's own docstring (`compare.py`) makes this degenerate versus non-degenerate argument for subsampling, but the noise-floor function does not apply it. With a fixed finite reference the statistic is only partly degenerate, so the truth lies between the two factors. That spans up to 1/√0.36 ≈ 1.67× in sd at n=256 (f=0.64) and about 1.21× at n=128 (f=0.32). The bias depends on n, so it contaminates exactly the n=128 versus n=256 comparisons (the "2.2×" in D.2/D.3). Supp. D.1 reports a check: declared units match fresh real draws to within 7%. The paper does not say where fresh, non-overlapping n=256 draws come from when the test split has 399 builds.

### W2. The statistical inference is described inconsistently, and parts of it are anti-conservative by the paper's own analysis.

- **Main and supplement name different tests.** Main §5 (l.368-369) says "significance comes from a paired bootstrap with Holm correction". Supp. D.4 says an exact label-permutation test replaced the bootstrap. Supp. Tab. 9 still says "paired bootstrap, 95% intervals". The code's `stats.py` docstring warns that with-replacement bootstrap is invalid for unbiased kernel statistics.
- **The intervals contradict the null claim.** The paired intervals for exterior MV-KID exclude zero: +0.2 [0.1, 0.3] (Supp. Tab. 9) and +1.1 [0.8, 1.4] (main Fig. 2). Yet the paper claims FILL-INTERIOR and REAL "share a significance group" (permutation p 0.61 to 0.77, Supp. D.4). Under the deployed translucent renderer a small real effect is expected (91% of views change, Spearman 0.96 per-build feature shift, §6.2). The honest statement is "detected at about 0.2 to 1 floor", not "not distinguished".
- **Non-rejection is used as evidence of blindness.** No equivalence test (TOST) or margin is given. Fig. 1c's ±2 band is a visual device, not a test. Outside the opaque-renderer theorem, the claim needs a stated margin.
- **The rank intervals are too narrow.** Table 4's rank intervals ("1,000 subsample replicates that subsample 80% of every arm", Supp. D.3) and the 40-generator intervals (Supp. L.3, "80%, 2,000 replicates") are the unrescaled spread of m-out-of-n subsamples. By the code's own derivation (`compare.py`), the true SE is about 2× this spread for non-degenerate differences (√(m/(n−m)) at frac=0.8), and the spread differs again in the degenerate regime. So "ties" (order flips in ≥5% of replicates) are under-detected, and the rank intervals are too narrow by about half.
- **The 40-generator pair tests appear to use a test the paper discredits.** Supp. L.3 says "Pair tests are Holm-corrected over all 780 pairs" with intervals from subsampling, and MV-KID replicates "drawn independently from each card's interval". If the 388 "both separated" pairs come from those replicates, they rest on the subsampling test that Supp. D.4 reports rejects 3.4× to 7.2× too often. Synthesizing MV-KID replicates from a CI ignores the shared-reference correlation and the non-normality of the null. The paper calls this "conservative", but only for the MV-KID side.
- **The permutation null tests something narrower than what is reported.** It tests "the two arms are one distribution", while the reported quantity is a difference of distances to a reference. The code docstring states this; the paper does not.

### W3. The 40-generator rank-correlation study does not support "the blindness reorders real generators" as stated.

- **The generators are not independent.** 22 are autoregressive variants of one model and 10 are facade variants, all from one project (acknowledged in Supp. L.3, "Scope"). The interval [0.42, 0.54] treats them as exchangeable units. A family-level or cluster-bootstrap analysis is needed.
- **The arms are scored at mixed n.** 35 are at n=256 and five LLM arms at n=128 to 133. If the ranking is in z-units, this is invalid by D.2. If it is in raw MMD², say so. Restricting to n=256 already moves ρ from 0.50 to 0.60 and reversals from 76 to 55 (Supp. Tab. 18).
- **Disagreement is partly true by construction.** V-KID is provably material-blind (Lemma 4) and MV-KID reads appearance, so ρ < 1 would hold even if interiors did not exist. The analysis establishes that the two metrics disagree, not that the render ranking is wrong. Only the facade subset (38 of 76 reversals) is tied to interiors. The other 38 are unexplained.
- **The headline depends on descriptor version.** Under the 80-d descriptor the reversals become 46 of 405 and ρ becomes 0.65 (Supp. L.3, last paragraph). The paper does not say which version is canonical.
- **There is no external ground truth.** The paper should correlate per-generator rank changes with an independent interior quantity: hidden-block fraction, sealed-interior volume, or the cutaway-view MV-KID it already has.

### W4. There is circularity and post-hoc design between V-KID, the gates and the leaderboard.

- **The near-twin gate was added after seeing the one external generator.** Supp. L.1 says "Neither novelty gate saw this, so we added a within-set near-twin gate". It disqualifies Dream-Cubed, whose BlockScore of 165.71 would otherwise beat every model the authors trained (229 to 345). It also disqualifies the vanilla templates (202.95, "ahead of every model we trained") and the two parametric baselines. The evidence for repetition is strong (59.8% versus 0.8%, 28 copies of one hut, size-matched 73% versus 0%). Still, a gate designed after inspecting a competitor's output is the textbook forking-paths problem. Its spread floor (0.02) and bar (3 spreads) are unjustified, and at n=128 the board is ungated (Supp. Tab. 5). Dream-Cubed is also a village generator scored against a house reference.
- **Some gate failures are true by construction.** Any appearance-reading encoder fails the geometry-tier invariance gate (G6, material rungs). By Lemma 3, any exterior observation cannot pass interior ordering. "Of fourteen structural metrics only V-KID passes every gate" (§6.1, l.419-422) is thus partly a restatement of the gate definitions. It is also contradicted by Supp. Tab. 15, where three random 3D CNNs, hashed 3³ patterns and multi-scale counts all pass every gate. The finding is really "any orbit-averaged occupancy KID passes", which is fine but should be stated that way.
- **The threshold timing claims cannot be verified.** Supp. C.3 says the thresholds were committed four weeks before the first voxel metric and the gate code has been unchanged for 58 days. The anonymized artifact should carry commit hashes or timestamps. The threshold sweep (Fig. 5) partly mitigates this, and I credit it.
- **A cheap control is missing.** A color-free Uni3D/ULIP-2 on interior-inclusive or volume points would likely pass G6. It is the natural test of whether V-KID adds anything over a learned 3D encoder once color is removed.

### W5. The kernel, preprocessing and dimensionality choices inflate V-KID's z relative to MV-KID, and the baseline statistics are tested where they are known to break.

- **The cubic kernel is effectively linear here.** Every feature row is L2-normalized and γ = 1/D (Supp. A; `distances.kid`, `geometry.Standardizer`). So k(a,b) = (1 + aᵀb/D)³ with |aᵀb| ≤ 1, and MMD² ≈ (3/D)‖μ_P − μ_Q‖² + O(1/D²). Both KIDs are thus close to linear-kernel mean-embedding tests: unwhitened DINO CLS means for MV-KID, and reference-whitened, ±5σ-clipped descriptor means for V-KID. Two consequences follow. (a) Second-order differences such as diversity or mode collapse barely register, which is why separate gates are needed; the paper should say so. (b) The z of a mean-difference test is roughly n‖Δμ‖²/√(2 tr Σ²), so it depends on effective dimension and on whitening. V-KID whitens against the reference. That magnifies directions in which real houses hardly vary: the thickness ≥5 bin has reference sd 1.6×10⁻⁴, so FILL-INTERIOR moves it 67.5 sd before clipping (Supp. A). MV-KID gets no whitening. Part of the "444 versus 0.35" gap is this preprocessing asymmetry. It cannot explain the opaque-renderer zero, but it matters for MATERIAL-SHUFFLE versus FILL-INTERIOR and for BlockScore's pillar balance.
- **The render-tier gate ladder runs at n=64 with 768-d features (Supp. Tab. 1).** Fréchet distance there requires covariance shrinkage. The code uses shrinkage 0.1 (`distances.fd`), which the paper does not disclose. FD's failure of n-stability is the well-known bias (Chong and Forsyth), and PRDC with k=5 at n=64 is known to be high-variance. The gates therefore rediscover known small-n pathologies rather than testing the statistics on fair terms. FID∞, FLD, CMMD (RBF on CLIP) and FD on PCA-reduced features at n≥256 are missing.
- **The arithmetic precision is not stated.** Kernel sums near 1 are differenced at the 10⁻⁶ level (raw MV-KID ×10³ = 0.001). Please state that the arithmetic is float64 for DINO features too. (The −4×10⁻¹³ episode in Supp. C.4 shows precision matters.)

### W6. BlockScore's worst-pillar rule is a power-weighted maximum with no external validation.

- **The maximum picks the most powerful test.** Taking the max of z-scores selects whichever metric has the smallest null variance at this n, not the most severe quality failure. Because the pillar floors scale differently with n (0.72 versus 0.49), the identity of the worst pillar can flip with n or with reference size. Table 5 does not report the worst-pillar identity at n=128.
- **Two of five pillars never act.** Their units are 29× and 52× coarser (Supp. D.1), so BlockScore is in practice max(appearance, geometry, palette). Calling it a five-pillar score overstates it.
- **The null is not zero and the score is not a p-value.** E[BlockScore | fresh real] = 1.08 with a skewed tail (Supp. D.1). That is acknowledged, but the leaderboard ranks probes, baselines and submissions together on this number.
- **There is no external evidence for max over mean.** No human, task (traversability) or downstream criterion supports the max rule. "The property we defend is the maximum" (l.304) is an argument, not an evaluation.

### W7. Sample sizes and observer studies are thin where they carry claims.

- **Objaverse is small.** It uses n=96 against 130 reference objects from a 290-object pool, so floor draws overlap heavily and the FPC issue of W1 is worse. V-KID on fill reads +73.8 at seed 0 and +118.4 at seed 1, a 60% seed swing. The voxelization resolution for meshes is not given.
- **The GPT-4o judge is dominated by position.** It picks the first slot 93% of the time and both orders agree on only 13% of exterior pairs (Supp. I.3). So "0.53, at chance" is mainly a position effect and is weak evidence of blindness. Report P(prefers real) restricted to order-consistent pairs, or a position-debiased Bradley-Terry fit. n also varies between conditions (256 versus 128).
- **The gate ladder runs at n=64 or n=128 while the board runs at n=256.** The gate verdicts that admit V-KID are thus established at a different n than the rankings they license.

### W8. The manuscript is not in submission state.

Visible draft annotations appear in the main PDF:

- "Comment: What does this mean haha?" at l.064-065.
- Further "Comment:" notes at l.069, 079, 096 (§5 l.343) and 113 (Related Work).
- "Comment: check all appendix refs" at l.221.

Supp. M contains "[TODO: Add the anonymized code URL...]". These are a desk-reject risk and must be removed. Several statements read as process narration ("we added a ... gate", "both are replaced"). The LLM statement says every numeral is re-derived by a registered check, yet the inconsistencies in §6 below show the check does not cover cross-section agreement.

## 4. Questions for the authors

1. Where do the "fresh real draws" that validate the n=256 floor (Supp. D.1, within 7%) come from, given that the test split has 399 builds? Did you compare √(1−f) with (1−f) corrections against non-overlapping draws, for example from the training split?
2. Which test produced each "separated" or "not separated" statement, including the 388 and 76 pairs, the Table 9 intervals and the Fig. 2 intervals: permutation, paired bootstrap or subsampling?
3. In the 40-generator study, is ranking done on raw MMD² or on z? How are the n=128 LLM arms handled? What do ρ and the reversal count become under exact permutation tests at equal n, and at family level?
4. What is BlockScore's worst-pillar identity per arm at n=128, and how many arms change worst pillar between n=128 and n=256?
5. How does V-KID behave with an RBF kernel (median heuristic), with a linear kernel, and without reference whitening? How does MV-KID behave with reference-whitened DINO features?
6. Do color-free Uni3D/ULIP-2 on volume points pass all gates?
7. Was the near-twin gate's bar (3 spreads, floor 0.02) fixed before or after seeing Dream-Cubed's 59.8%? What does the leaderboard look like without it?
8. What is the equivalence margin under which you claim FILL-INTERIOR is indistinguishable under the deployed renderer, and what is the TOST result?

## 5. Rating

**Borderline Reject (3 of 6). Confidence 4 of 5.**

The empirical core is convincing and the controls are better than most metric papers. The opaque-renderer impossibility is close to tautological (Lemma 1 is self-described as one). The value therefore lies in the quantification and the protocol, and both are undermined by the issues above. The unit of quantification is used across n, runs and metrics in ways the paper itself forbids. The significance machinery is described inconsistently and is partly anti-conservative. The external-generator conclusion rests on a post-hoc gate. Most of this is fixable with CPU-only reanalysis of cached features within the week. If W1, W2 and W8 are fixed and W3 and W4 are reframed, I would move to Borderline or Weak Accept.

## 7. Factual inconsistencies (page / section / line)

1. **Significance method.** Main §5, p.5, l.368-369 says "paired bootstrap with Holm". Supp. D.4, p.7, l.363-376 says exact permutation, with the bootstrap replaced. Supp. Tab. 9 caption, p.11, says "paired bootstrap, 95% intervals".
2. **Exterior DINOv2-B separation in the 512-reference ablation.** Main Fig. 2, p.6 says "+1.1 [0.8, 1.4]". Supp. Tab. 9, p.11 says +0.2 [0.1, 0.3]. Supp. Fig. 1 caption, p.2 says "row 1 scores +0.2". Main §6.2, l.437-438 says "between 0.0 and 0.2". Both intervals also exclude 0, which contradicts "indistinguishable" and "same significance group" (abstract l.012-014, §1 l.062-066).
3. **Cutaway separations.** Main Fig. 2, p.6 gives +9.1 / +20.3 / +56.5 for facing quadrant, roof off and floor plan. Supp. Tab. 9 gives +6.7 / +17.1 / +55.8. Main §1 l.074 and §6.2 l.444 say "6.7 to 55.8". Main Tab. 2 says +55.8. Supp. Fig. 1 says "+56".
4. **Exterior pixel difference.** Main Fig. 2 panel shows 0.89/255. Main Fig. 2 caption says 3.9/255. Supp. Fig. 1 says "0.2/255 per frame". These are possibly different builds, but none is labelled.
5. **HOLLOW-SHELL V-KID at n=256.** Main Tab. 2 and §6.3 l.478 give +231.1 (converse run). Supp. Tab. 10 and Tab. 15 give +265.4 (leaderboard). Main Tab. 2 mixes the two runs in one column (FILL +444.36 from the leaderboard).
6. **MATERIAL-SHUFFLE and MONOCHROME, corner-view MV-KID.** Main Tab. 4 gives +422.43 / +535.36. Supp. Tab. 10 gives +346 / +439. Supp. G.2 (l.489-491) says the n=256 leaderboard's builds and noise floor are held fixed. Both ratios are about 1.22, which suggests a different floor.
7. **BlockScore pillar units.** Main §4.4 l.310-312 says three pillars are in true floors and two in a coarser unit. Main Tab. 4 caption says "the three that never determine it here use a coarser in-run scale". Supp. D.1 lists palette as a true-floor pillar.
8. **VOXEL-AR-U rank at n=256.** Main Tab. 4 and Supp. D.3 (l.347) say 4th. Supp. L.2 l.1035 says "ranks fifth".
9. **Supp. Fig. 12 (p.23) ranks.** It numbers arms 1, 2, 4, 5, 6, 8, 11 and 13. It shows Dream-Cubed as rank 2 with BlockScore 165.7 and GABLED-HOUSE as rank 11. Main Tab. 4 lists both as DQ and gives ranks 3, 4, 5-7 and 10.
10. **Holm groups.** Supp. Tab. 17 (p.19) gives the contaminated arm "c", which it shares with clean VOXEL-AR "cd" and LLM-FT "cd". Supp. Fig. 10B (p.20) gives the contaminated arm "d" alone and clean, LLM-FT and LLM-FT-GEO all "e". Fig. 10 thus implies the contaminated arm is separated from its clean retrain, which Tab. 17's caption denies.
11. **V-KID cost.** Main §4.2 l.272-273 says "about a minute per arm at n=64 on CPU". Supp. I.2 l.748 and Supp. M say 15 to 18 s for about 3,000 to 4,400 builds on one CPU process.
12. **Null readings.** Main §4.3 l.291-293 says the unbiased estimator "reads 0.000 at every n" and the biased one 0.071 at n=64. Supp. Tab. 1 (n=64) gives KID real 0.011 and biased MMD real 0.079. The conventions (disjoint halves versus test-vs-reference) need labels.
13. **Supp. Tab. 2.** Main §6.1 l.402-407 cites it as the render-statistic ladder in a Minecraft context. Supp. Tab. 2 is the LEGO (StableText2Brick) ladder at n=128. There FILL-INTERIOR reads KID 2.0 and MMD-RBF 2.1, while main Tab. 3 gives the same domain's FILL-INTERIOR MV-KID as −0.09 (ST2B) and −0.01 (ShapeNet).
14. **FILL-INTERIOR V-KID at n=128.** It appears as 111.4 (Supp. Tab. 12), 201.8 (Supp. Tab. 5 and 14, Fig. 2, Fig. 4) and 286.7/287 (Supp. D.2, Tab. 8, main §6.1 l.395). Supp. D.2 l.318 gives a twelve-seed range of 185 to 274. Two of the three values lie outside it, and the runs are not labelled by reference size.
15. **Floor scaling.** Main Tab. 4 caption says the deviation "halves when n doubles". Supp. D.2 gives an MV-KID ratio of 0.72 (n=64→128) and Supp. D.3 gives 0.56 (0.00289→0.00161).
16. **Scope of the bit-identity claim.** Main §6.2 l.455-458 says "Rendered with glass opaque, all 1,596 test-split views are bit-identical ... and MV-KID is exactly 0". The Supp. H table (p.11) shows that glass opaque alone gives 8.1% bit-identical (per-build atlas) or 88.9% (GPU raster). Bit-identity needs a fixed atlas plus CPU raster.
