# CVPR 2027 Review: "A Building Is Not Its Silhouette: Structural Blindness in 3D Generation Evaluation"

Reviewer background: 3D generation (mesh / NeRF / 3DGS / sparse-voxel generators) and their evaluation practice (FID/KID on renders, CLIP score, CD/EMD with COV/MMD/1-NNA, Uni3D/ULIP scores, GPTEval3D-style VLM judges).

Material read: main paper in full (8 pages + LLM statement + refs); supplement Secs. A, B, C.3, C.4, D.1–D.2, G, H, I.1–I.3, J, K, L.1, L.3.

---

## 1. Summary

The paper argues that render-and-embed metrics (multi-view DINOv2/CLIP/Inception KID or FID), point-cloud metrics on visible surfaces, and a GPT-4o judge on exterior views cannot see the interiors of 3D structures. It probes this with complement probes on real Minecraft houses: FILL-INTERIOR, which fills every sealed air cell with the build's dominant material (median +33% blocks), and HOLLOW-SHELL, which deletes every block not touching outside air. Multi-view DINOv2 KID (MV-KID) places both probes within about 0.35 "noise floors" of real data, while a material permutation lands at +422. The null holds over five encoders, up to 16 views, perspective projection, Uni3D/ULIP-2/Chamfer/EMD on visible-surface points, LEGO (ShapeNet brick abstractions) and 290 Objaverse multi-room buildings. Cutaway views with the same encoder recover the signal (7 to 56 floors). The authors propose V-KID, an unbiased KID on a 77-d hand-designed D4-invariant voxel descriptor (2×2×2 pattern orbits, wall-thickness histogram, height profile, 9 scalars) that separates FILL-INTERIOR by 444 floors and is exactly blind to material changes. They also propose a gating protocol (ordering, invariance, n-stability) under which Fréchet distance, biased MMD, PRDC and coverage fail, plus BlockScore, a max over five pillars with memorization and mode-collapse disqualification. Over 40 generators (all but one or two from the authors' own project), MV-KID and V-KID rankings correlate at ρ=0.50 and reverse 76 significantly separated pairs.

## 2. Strengths

1. **Clean, well-controlled probe design.** The complement probes are well built: same-material fill, the HOLLOW-SHELL exact complement, the mass-matched GROW-SHELL control (+102 vs +0.3 under MV-KID, with block counts matched to one block), and the opaque-renderer check showing bit-identical renders. GROW-SHELL in particular rules out "it's just block count" cleanly. This is the most convincing experiment in the paper.
2. **The positive control is the right one.** The same encoder and statistic with only the cameras changed (Fig. 2, Supp. Tab. 9) pins the failure on the observation, not the encoder. That is a cleaner argument than most metric-critique papers make.
3. **Breadth of observers.** Five 2D encoders, 4/8/16 views, Uni3D and ULIP-2 under three samplers, Chamfer/EMD with MMD/COV/1-NNA, voxel IoU, and a VLM judge. Supp. Tab. 12 is the table I would have asked for: the standard voxel/point-cloud suite either misses the probe or *rewards* the fill (IoU/CD nearest-neighbour MMD rate filled houses closer to real than real). That last result is a useful, non-obvious finding for the voxel-generation community.
4. **Statistical hygiene above the field's norm.** Unbiased estimators, paired bootstrap with Holm correction, explicit noise floors, group-aware splits (with the 28.7% family-leakage measurement), and a documented contamination case. The robustness study of V-KID (family drop-out, random subsets, random 3D CNN features in Supp. Sec. J) answers the "hand-tuned to the probe" objection better than I expected.
5. **Honest limitations.** The supplement documents its own gate bugs (C.4), the non-commensurate BlockScore pillars (D.1), and the VLM's position bias (I.3).

## 3. Weaknesses (ranked by impact on my score)

### W1. The core finding is close to obvious, and the paper's framing makes it look more so.
"A metric computed on exterior renders cannot see occluded interiors" is something most 3D-vision readers will accept without an experiment. The paper's own "theorem" (Supp. H, Lemma 1) is that identical renders give identical metric values, which is a tautology, not a result. Presenting the opaque-renderer case as "provable" and the main headline as "every observer misses the interior" leans on the least informative part of the work. The parts that are *not* obvious, namely (a) translucent-renderer leaks reaching single-image features but not the set statistic (Spearman 0.96 per-house, <1.4 floors per set), (b) standard 3D metrics (IoU/CD-MMD) *rewarding* filled interiors, (c) ranking reversals among real generators, and (d) the mass-matched GROW-SHELL dissociation, are scattered and mostly in the supplement. As written, a reviewer skimming Fig. 1 and the abstract concludes "they confirmed occlusion exists."

### W2. Relevance beyond Minecraft is asserted more than demonstrated, and the "corruption" is not obviously damage outside Minecraft.
- For LEGO and Objaverse, the paper shows metrics are blind to filling hidden volume, but never shows that a filled interior is *worse* in those domains. A solid LEGO model is still buildable and arguably more stable. A filled Objaverse building is a perfectly good rendering asset. The ordering gate treats FILL-INTERIOR as "damage of known severity", but that severity is a domain value judgement that holds for walkable Minecraft houses and not obviously elsewhere.
- No mainstream 3D generator is evaluated. The conclusion says "the same audit is owed to mesh and implicit 3D generation", and §6.3 says "surface-fitted mesh and implicit generators emit hollow shells", but no TRELLIS / XCube / LT3SD / SemCity / BlockFusion / Hunyuan3D / sparse-voxel output is ever scored. The 3D-native domain where interiors clearly matter (indoor scene synthesis: ATISS, DiffuScene, Holodeck, LayoutGPT, and semantic-scene SSC/generation such as SemCity, XCube, LT3SD) is not discussed. That literature already evaluates on top-down floor-plan renders, which are cutaways, so the claim that "the field" evaluates interiors only through exterior views is too broad.
- V-KID needs a voxel grid with well-defined inside/outside. For non-watertight meshes and 3DGS (most of the field), "sealed interior" is ill-defined, and the paper offers no recipe beyond voxelization.
Net: this reads as a strong Minecraft/procedural-content evaluation paper with a LEGO side study. CVPR's 3D audience will see it as niche unless the generalization is made concrete.

### W3. Claims overreach the evidence in several specific places.
- **"Across 40 generators"** (abstract): per Supp. L.3, all 40 come from one project (22 AR variants of the authors' model, 10 facade variants, 5 LLM-program arms, two 3D-BPE models, BrickNet). These are not 40 independent generators; 24 of the 76 reversals come from one family. The abstract should say so.
- **"Geometric gap 4.4 times its render gap" (BrickGPT)** and **"a factor of 1,270 between two pillars"**: these are ratios of z-scores in two metrics' own noise floors. The paper itself shows (Supp. D.2) that a noise-floor separation scales roughly linearly in n for V-KID and not for MV-KID (0.49 vs 0.72 ratios), and changes with reference size (444 vs 120.8 for the same probe at the same n). A cross-metric ratio of such numbers has no stable meaning. These headline ratios should go.
- **Two-decimal precision** ("+444.36 noise floors", "+422.43") while the unit itself has ~10% relative SE (Supp. D.2). This is false precision.
- **The GPT-4o evidence is weaker than presented.** Supp. I.3 reports that on exterior pairs the judge picks the first slot 93% of the time and the two orders agree on only 13% of pairs. "0.53, at chance" is therefore mostly position bias, not evidence that a VLM is blind to interiors. The roof-off 0.70 is the informative number. The main text and Fig. 1c present the 0.53 as a clean null.
- **Dream-Cubed disqualification**: the one external Minecraft generator is a village/terrain-chunk model scored against a house reference, then disqualified by a within-set near-twin gate the authors added *after* seeing its outputs ("Neither novelty gate saw this, so we added..."). Even if the gate is sensible, a post-hoc gate that removes the only external competitor will read as a red flag, and the comparison is out of domain to begin with.
- **"The blindness reorders real generators"**: true for the authors' facade generators, which emit shells. But no evidence shows that the V-KID order is the *correct* one (see W4).

### W4. No evidence that V-KID tracks quality; it is validated only against synthetic corruptions it was partly designed around.
V-KID's descriptor includes a wall-thickness histogram and a sealed-interior scalar, which directly encode the probe (Supp. A: one thickness bin moves 67.5 reference SDs under FILL-INTERIOR). The ablations (J.1, J.2) show the separation survives removing these, and random 3D CNN features also separate the probe. That defuses tuning, but it also shows the converse: **any volumetric feature sees interiors**, so the 444 says little about V-KID specifically. What is missing is evidence that V-KID orders *generators* the way a human (or a functional test) would. The only external signal, the GPT-4o Bradley–Terry ranking, correlates 0.20 with V-KID versus 0.74 with MV-KID, and it ranks real data sixth. The authors dismiss the judge as "a third exterior observer", which is fair for exterior views, but then no interior-aware judge (human with cutaways, or a VLM on cutaways at the system level) is run. Without one, "V-KID is right and MV-KID is wrong on the 76 reversed pairs" is an assumption. A human 2AFC on cutaway views of reversed pairs, or a functional metric (enterability, navigable floor area, connected rooms), is the experiment a metric paper at CVPR needs.

### W5. Contribution 3 (gates) largely restates known results, and the BlockScore machinery dilutes the paper.
That biased MMD decays with n, and FID is biased in n, are established (Bińkowski et al. 2018 introduced KID for exactly this; Chong & Forsyth 2020). That precision/recall/density are not monotone under some corruptions is also documented. The gating protocol is reasonable practice but not a CVPR-level contribution by itself. BlockScore adds a max over five pillars, two of which are in a unit 29–52× coarser and "never determine the maximum" (Supp. D.1), so in practice it is max(MV-KID z, V-KID z). Its null is not zero, it cannot be read as a p-value, and it is uninterpretable without the pillar column. The leaderboard, contamination case study, Dream-Cubed extraction, near-twin gate, and 13 Supp. sections take space from the one strong message.

### W6. The noise-floor unit makes magnitudes hard to interpret and compare.
Separations in "noise floors" grow linearly with n for V-KID (degenerate U-statistic null) and depend on reference size. So "444" is a sample-size artifact as much as an effect size. The paper is candid about this (D.2), but then builds headline claims on these magnitudes. A 3D-vision reader would prefer an n-free quantity: raw MMD² with CI, an effect size normalized by between-build spread, or most usefully **the minimum n at which each metric detects each probe at α=0.05** (a detection-power curve). That would make the MV-KID vs V-KID contrast stark and n-independent.

### W7. Presentation: the submitted PDF contains author-to-author comments and inconsistencies.
- Inline comments are visible in the main paper: "Comment: What does this mean haha? These two last lines" (l.64), "Comment: weakish? Hard to follow..." (l.69), "Comment: what does this line mean" (l.79), "Comment: review the flow here" (l.113), "Comment: check all appendix refs" (l.221), "Comment: what is this next line?" (l.342). In a real review this alone signals an unfinished submission. It must not ship.
- Fig. 2 vs text and tables: the figure reports exterior separation +1.1 [0.8,1.4] and floor-plan +56.5, with a pixel difference of 0.89/255 on the panel. The caption says 3.9/255, Supp. Tab. 9 says +0.2 for DINOv2-B exterior, and §6.2/Table 2 say floorplan +55.8. These look like numbers from different runs. Reconcile them, or state which run each comes from.
- The intro opener ("Many of us are taught at a young age that we shouldn't judge a book by its cover") is informal for CVPR.
- The main text points to the supplement constantly (Supp. Secs. A–M, more than 40 forward references). Several load-bearing results live only there: Tab. 12 (3D metrics rewarding fill), GROW-SHELL details, the 40-generator analysis.
- Table 4 is dense: BlockScore, worst pillar, two z columns, two raw columns, and DQ rows with heterogeneous reasons. The point (complementary blindness) is better carried by a 2×2 "probe × metric" panel.

### W8. Missing related work and baselines.
- **3D-native distribution metrics with learned 3D features**: Fréchet PointNet Distance (FPD, Shu et al. 2019), P-FID/P-KID (Point-E, Nichol et al. 2022), and the voxel-classifier Inception scores used in early voxel GANs (Wu et al. 2016 3D-GAN; Xie et al. 3D descriptor nets). These are the closest prior "volumetric KID", and V-KID should be positioned against them. A 3D-CNN-feature KID on occupancy (the paper's random 3D CNN is close) is the natural learned baseline and should appear in the main paper.
- **Shading-image FID / rendering-based 3D FID** (SDF-StyleGAN, GET3D, LION evaluation) and **text-to-3D evaluation suites** (T3Bench, GPTEval3D [cited], 3DGen-Bench, Hi3DEval, MATE-3D), several of which already discuss render-only blind spots.
- **Indoor scene synthesis and semantic-scene generation** (ATISS, DiffuScene, LayoutGPT, Holodeck; SemCity, XCube, LT3SD, BlockFusion) where interiors are the object of study and the evaluation conventions differ (top-down renders, collision/walkable-area metrics). This is the most relevant "interior matters" community and is absent.
- **Functional/physical metrics** for generated assemblies (BrickGPT's own stability and buildability, physics-based plausibility checks). These are the "measure structure directly" alternative to a distributional descriptor.

## 4. Questions to the authors

1. On the 76 reversed generator pairs, which ranking do humans prefer when shown cutaway views? Do you have any interior-aware human or functional signal (enterability, navigable area, room count) that V-KID tracks better than MV-KID?
2. Why is FILL-INTERIOR damage in LEGO and Objaverse? What downstream property gets worse for a filled ShapeNet brick model or a filled Objaverse building?
3. Can you report, for each metric and probe, the minimum n at which the probe is detected at α=0.05? That would replace the n-dependent "noise floor" magnitudes.
4. How many of the 40 generators are external to your project, and does ρ=0.50 hold when the facade family is excluded? (Supp. Tab. 18 suggests ρ=0.37 among AR models, which is even lower, so this would help.)
5. Was the within-set near-twin gate specified before scoring Dream-Cubed? If not, how does the paper's conclusion change if Dream-Cubed is ranked?
6. How does V-KID extend to non-watertight meshes or 3DGS, where "sealed air" is ill-defined? Have you run it on any surface-fitted generator's voxelized output?
7. Fig. 2 numbers (+1.1, +56.5, 0.89/255) disagree with Table 2, Tab. 9 and the caption. Which run are they from?
8. With exterior-pair position bias at 93% first-slot, what is GPT-4o's preference after order-debiasing (e.g., only pairs where both orders agree)?

## 5. Rating

**Weak Reject (2 on a 1–6 scale; borderline-reject region).** Confidence: 4/5.

The experimental work is careful and much more thorough than typical metric-critique papers, and several sub-findings are genuinely useful (IoU/CD-MMD rewarding filled interiors, mass-matched dissociation, translucency leaks lost at the set level). But the headline finding is close to self-evident, the evidence that the alternative metric is *better* rather than merely *different* is missing, generalization beyond Minecraft is asserted rather than shown on mainstream 3D generators, several headline numbers are cross-metric ratios of n-dependent units, and the PDF still contains author comments. With W1–W4 addressed, I would move to borderline/weak accept.

## 7. Specific probes requested

**Relevance beyond Minecraft.** Currently borderline niche. The LEGO and Objaverse experiments show *blindness* but not *harm*. The paper needs (i) one domain-general argument for why interiors matter (navigation/collision volumes for embodied AI, buildability/stability for assemblies), tied to a measurable downstream property, and (ii) one external non-Minecraft generator. Without those, CVPR reviewers outside procedural content will treat this as a games/PCG paper.

**Is the core finding obvious?** The headline (exterior views miss interiors) is obvious. The supporting findings are not, and they should lead: standard 3D metrics *prefer* filled interiors; per-image leaks wash out at the set level; mass-matched dissociation; and real design-decision verdicts flip.

**Overreach.** "40 generators" (one project), cross-metric z-ratios (4.4×, 1,270×), two-decimal z-scores with a ~10% unit SE, the GPT-4o null that is mostly position bias, a post-hoc gate disqualifying the sole external Minecraft generator, and "the same audit is owed to mesh and implicit 3D generation" with no mesh/implicit generator evaluated.

**Missing baselines / related work.** Learned volumetric KID (3D CNN on occupancy) in the main table; FPD and P-FID; shading-image FID; text-to-3D evaluation suites; indoor scene synthesis and semantic scene generation; functional metrics (stability, enterability, walkable area). Most important: some human or functional ground truth on generator pairs.

**Figures and tables.** Fig. 1 is effective: a real house and its filled twin, roof-off, plus a per-observer dot plot. Its log axis with a ±2 band is clear, although "noise floors" needs a one-line definition in the caption. Table 2 is the best table and carries the paper. Fig. 2 makes the right point but its numbers disagree with Tab. 9 and Table 2. Table 4 is overloaded, and its DQ block distracts from the complementarity it is meant to show. I would replace it with a compact probe × metric heatmap (rows: FILL, HOLLOW, GROW-SHELL, MATERIAL-SHUFFLE, MONOCHROME; columns: MV-KID, Uni3D-vis, Uni3D-all, CD-MMD, IoU-MMD, V-KID) and move the leaderboard to the supplement. A detection-n plot (n on the x-axis, rejection rate on the y-axis, one line per metric) would communicate the contrast better than any z-score.
