# AC + clarity critique: "A Building Is Not Its Silhouette" (CVPR 2027 draft, main.pdf 10 pp, supp.pdf 24 pp)

## 0. Blockers to fix before anything else (desk-reject / embarrassment risk)

1. **Inline editorial comments are typeset in the PDF.** "Comment: What does this mean haha? These two last lines" (l.64-65), "Comment: weakish? Hard to follow..." (l.69-70), "Comment: what does this line mean" (l.79), "Comment: review the flow here" (l.113), "Comment: check all appendix refs" (l.221-222), "Comment: what is this next line?" (l.342-343). The supp has "[TODO: Add the anonymized code URL and commit, and the institutional determination ...]" (supp l.1133-1135). Turn off the comment macro for the submission build and add a grep check to the Makefile that fails if "Comment:" or "TODO" shows up in pdftotext output.
2. **The same quantity has different numbers in the same place.** Fig. 2's in-figure label reads "pixel difference 0.89/255" and its caption says "mean pixel difference of 3.9/255". Fig. 2 gives the floor-plan cutaway as +56.5, Table 2 as +55.8 and Supp Fig. 1 as +56. §6.2 says cutaways recover "6.7 to 55.8", but no cutaway value in Fig. 2 or Table 2 is 6.7. FILL-INTERIOR under MV-KID appears as +0.35 (Fig. 1, Table 2, Table 4), +0.21 (§6.3), +0.3 (Grow-Shell run), +1.1 (Fig. 2), +0.2 (Supp Fig. 1), and 0.48/0.34 (dose, §6.1). Each one is defensible because it comes from a different run, but a reviewer sees a paper whose headline number changes from page to page. Pick one canonical run for each claim, say which run every other number comes from, and make the figure labels match the captions.
3. **Page budget.** The conclusion ends on p.8 and the LLM Usage Statement takes the top of p.9, before the references. Check the CVPR 2027 author kit. If extra statements count toward the 8 pages, the paper is over length. Move the statement after the references, or into supp, if that is allowed.

## 1. The AC's view

**My one-sentence take after 5 minutes:** "Render-based metrics for voxel and brick generators cannot see interiors, which is true by construction. The authors quantify this with a fill-the-interior probe, propose a hand-crafted voxel-occupancy KID (V-KID) that can see interiors, and wrap both in a gated leaderboard protocol."

**Is that what the authors claim?** Mostly. They claim three contributions: (1) interior blindness, located in the observation; (2) V-KID; (3) a validation protocol with gates. The gap is in emphasis. The authors present (1) as the discovery. A CVPR reader will think (1) is obvious, especially since the paper itself proves it as a theorem for opaque renderers (§6.2, l.452-458). The novelty that survives is (2)+(3) plus the evidence that the blindness changes real rankings (40 generators, 76 reversed pairs). That evidence is the most important result, and it gets one paragraph (l.532-539). Its strongest caveat ("The 40 generators come from one project", supp l.1115) appears only in supp.

**Is the significance clear outside Minecraft/LEGO?** Only partly. The intro motivates with LEGO, Minecraft and "indoor environments for robotic training" (l.37-39), but no indoor-scene or robotics experiment follows. The Objaverse result (290 buildings, l.493-497) is the only non-blocky domain, and it gets three lines. The conclusion says "the same audit is owed to mesh and implicit 3D generation" (l.609). But mesh and implicit generators output surfaces, so for them "interior" means the voxelized inside of a watertight shell. That case is HOLLOW-SHELL, and the paper never runs it on a mesh generator. A mainstream 3D-generation reviewer (TRELLIS, Hunyuan3D, etc.) will ask: "why should I care, given my outputs have no interior?" The paper needs an explicit answer, such as "anything that is assembled, simulated, navigated or 3D-printed has an interior", backed by one measured functional consequence.

**Likely reviewer consensus weaknesses:**
- **W1, "obvious by construction".** An opaque exterior render cannot see sealed voids, and the paper proves it. Seven statistics, five encoders, 16 views and GPT-4o confirm a tautology. The extra encoders and views read as over-investment in the trivial direction.
- **W2, no external validity for V-KID.** V-KID is validated only against synthetic corruptions that the authors chose, and those corruptions target exactly the property V-KID was built to read (thickness bins, a sealed-interior scalar). The ablation that removes the size term and sealed scalar (l.540-546) and the GROW-SHELL mass-matched control (l.547-557) are the right answers to this, but they come late. There is no human or functional evidence that V-KID's ordering of *generators* is more correct than MV-KID's. GPT-4o "ranks held-out real sixth of eleven" (l.429-430), so the one judge in the paper is not usable as ground truth either.
- **W3, generator evidence is narrow and in-house.** The only external Minecraft generator (Dream-Cubed) is trained on villages, scored against houses, and then disqualified. BrickGPT is the only external generator that gets ranked. Its V-KID gap (+51.5) is close to the solidified-real control (+36.4), and the abstract's "geometric gap 4.4 times its render gap" ratio has no clear meaning, because z-scores from different features and different power are not commensurable. All 40 generators in the rank-correlation analysis come from one project.
- **W4, V-KID is a hand-crafted 77-d descriptor.** It is local-occupancy statistics, and by the authors' own admission it is blind to global coherence (PATCH-STITCH, l.574-579) and to materials. Reviewers will see it as one reasonable descriptor among many. The random-3D-CNN result (supp J) helps but is buried.
- **W5, the protocol pieces are known.** Gates on ordering, invariance and n-stability restate Borji, O'Bray and Chong & Forsyth (the authors say so, l.283). FID's n-dependence and the biased-MMD problem are textbook (Bińkowski 2018). "Fréchet distance fails" in the abstract will read as a rediscovery.
- **W6, readability.** The paper is dense with private vocabulary and with numbers that have no reference point (see §2).

**Likely decision: borderline reject (scores around 3/3/4 out of 6) in the current form.** The idea is clean and the experiments are thorough. The engineering (in-run noise floors, group-aware split, contamination catch) is careful. But the headline finding is perceived as obvious, the fix is a hand-designed descriptor with no human or functional validation, and the clarity problems make reviewers work hard. It would flip to weak accept if (a) the framing leads with "this reorders real models, and here is a functional or human check showing which order is right", and (b) the comments, inconsistencies and jargon are cleaned up. Presentation alone will not flip it. W2 needs some new evidence.

## 2. Clarity and presentation critique

### Jargon and undefined or inconsistent terms
| Term | Where | Problem |
|---|---|---|
| "noise floor(s)" as a unit | Abstract l.12-14, everywhere | The unit is a null-standardized z-score, and Table 4's caption calls it exactly that. "Noise floor" usually means a threshold, not a unit, so "0.35 noise floors" and "444 noise floors" read oddly. Fig. 1's axis says "noise floors from real" with the shaded band at ±2. Pick one name ("null-z" or "σ₀ units"), define it once in §3, and use it everywhere. |
| "spreads" | l.528, Table 4 DQ rows ("+44.9 spreads", "+10.1 spreads") | A second, undefined unit. Define it or convert it. |
| "pillars", "five pillars" | l.302-313, Table 4 | The five pillars are never named in the main paper (only in Supp D.1). Table 4 shows two of them. Name all five in one sentence, or drop the word and say "BlockScore = max over five per-axis scores (appearance, geometry, ...)". |
| "tier" / "render tier" / "geometry tier" | l.276, 305, 451, 505 | Used interchangeably with "pillar" and "metric". Pick one. |
| "rungs", "ladder", "dose", "axis" | l.279, 289, 392-402 | Undefined metaphors. "Rungs of known damage" means corruption severity levels. Define "ladder = a corruption applied at increasing severity" in §4.1 and use it consistently. |
| "arm" | §3 l.192 | Defined, but it is RL/clinical-trial vocabulary that CVPR readers do not use. "Method" or "sample set" is more familiar. If you keep it, Table 1 should say "arms" in the header. |
| "converse-probe run", "protocol run", "head-to-head", "leaderboard run" | l.358, 359, 475 | These are internal run names. Replace them with "a separate run at n=256 that scores both probes". |
| "MV-DINO-KID" | Fig. 1c label | It is "MV-KID" everywhere else. |
| "twins", "near-twin gate" | l.527, Table 4 | Fine once defined, but the definition is buried in the Table 4 caption. |
| "the current cards", "80-dimensional descriptor" | Supp l.1122 | Leftover internal vocabulary, and it contradicts the 77-d description. Remove it or explain it. |
| "submissions" vs "baselines" | Table 1 vs Table 4 | Table 1 lists PATCH-STITCH and RANDOM-VOXELS as procedural baselines. Table 4 puts them under "submissions". |
| "significance group" | Abstract l.14, l.62-65 | Never defined (this is the line the authors' own comment flags). |

### Top 10 issues with concrete rewrites

**1. Abstract (l.001-030): it is a list of numbers, and the main point arrives late.** The reader gets 0.35, 33%, 444, 40, 0.50, 76 and 4.4 without knowing why each matters. Suggested rewrite of the structure:
> "Generative models of 3D structure are ranked by metrics that observe renders or visible surfaces, yet for buildings, LEGO models and indoor scenes the interior decides whether a structure can be entered, assembled or navigated. We show that this blindness changes conclusions. Filling every room of real Minecraft houses (+33% blocks) leaves a multi-view DINOv2 kernel distance within sampling noise of real data, across seven statistics, five encoders, point-cloud encoders and a GPT-4o judge, and in three domains. We introduce V-KID, a CPU-only voxel kernel distance that separates the same corruption by over 400 null standard deviations, and a protocol that admits a metric to a leaderboard only after it passes ordering, invariance and sample-size gates. Over 40 generators, render and voxel rankings correlate at only 0.50 and disagree on 76 significantly separated pairs, [and <functional/human check> agrees with V-KID on X%]."
Drop "4.4 times its render gap". Without context it carries no meaning.

**2. Intro opening (l.032-039): "Many of us are taught at a young age that we shouldn't judge a book by its cover."** This is a cliché, and it uses an en dash as a dash ("doors–an interior"). Open with the stakes instead: "A generated building that cannot be entered, a LEGO model that cannot be assembled and a scene a robot cannot navigate can all look perfect from outside. Yet the metrics that rank 3D generators look only from outside."

**3. Intro paragraph 2 (l.057-068): the probe result is stated through an undefined statistical procedure.** "share a significance group under a paired, Holm-corrected comparison over all arm pairs" should become "is statistically indistinguishable from real data (paired bootstrap, Holm-corrected), while the same metric separates a material permutation of the same builds by 422σ."

**4. Intro paragraphs 3-4 (l.069-086): defensive and repetitive.** "The failure is in what the views show, not in what the encoder computes" appears in some form five times in the paper (l.071, 446-449, 600, Fig. 2 title, §6.2 title). Say it once in the intro, with the theorem. Then **promote the GROW-SHELL mass-matched control into the intro**: it is the most convincing experiment you have ("same builds, same material, same added mass; only inside versus outside differs; MV-KID +102 vs +0.3"). At the moment it sits at l.547.

**5. Contributions (l.087-111): contribution 1 claims something that is provable, so a reviewer discounts it.** Reframe it as "We measure how large the blindness is and show it reorders real generators", and move the rank-reversal result into contribution 1. Contribution 3's list of failing metrics should cite Bińkowski and Chong & Forsyth inline, so it reads as "we operationalize known desiderata as hard gates", not as a new discovery.

**6. Fig. 1 (p.1): the panels are good, but the caption contradicts §6.2 and mixes runs.** "Under flat colours their exterior renders are bit-identical in all four metric views" makes a reader think the metric renders are flat. The default renderer is textured and translucent, and it alters 91% of views (l.459-460). Say so: "Under opaque rendering the exterior views are bit-identical; under our default renderer, glass leaks a few pixels (§6.2)". The caption also gives Uni3D at n=128 with "seeds −0.1 and +0.4" next to MV-KID and V-KID at n=256, while the paper elsewhere insists that magnitudes do not compare across runs. Either use one run or mark the Uni3D dot as coming from a different run. Rename "MV-DINO-KID" to MV-KID. The symlog x-axis ("0 10 100 1000") needs a note.

**7. Table 2 (p.6): the title "Every observer misses the interior" is contradicted by its own bottom half.** Four rows in the bottom half see the interior. Retitle it "Every exterior observer misses the interior; every interior-including observer sees it." The caption is a dense run of provenance ("MV-KID and V-KID: n=256 leaderboard, HOLLOW-SHELL from the converse-probe run at the same n; point clouds: n=128, seed 0 ..."). Move provenance into a footnote-sized line and lead with how to read the table: "values are null-z; |z|<2 is within noise". Show "—" consistently and say why entries are missing.

**8. Table 4 (p.8): it carries too much and its point is lost.** A reader cannot tell what to take away. Problems: probes sit in the ranking (ranks 5-7 are shared across sections); "(2)†" for a contaminated arm; the raw MMD² columns are in different units with no explanation of why both are there; the DQ rows mix two kinds of reason; and "1,270×" in the text (l.502) is a ratio whose denominator is inside the noise, so it means nothing. Suggestion: split it into (a) the ranked submissions only, with appearance, geometry and worst pillar, and (b) probes and DQs as a compact "the protocol catches these" panel. Delete the 1,270× ratio and say "inside noise vs. 444σ".

**9. §6.4 last paragraphs (l.518-569): the most important results are written as a run of findings with no signposting.** Dream-Cubed DQ, rank reversal, V-KID ablation, GROW-SHELL and the contamination story all share one subsection. Use bold run-in heads ("Rank reversals." "Is V-KID just counting blocks?" "Contamination.") and lead each with its claim. The reversal paragraph should include the reversed pair figure (Supp Fig. 11, facade vs. voxel model, with the roof-off view). A CVPR paper with only two figures, neither of which shows a generator's output, is visually underpowered.

**10. LLM Usage Statement (l.611-633) and reproducibility.** The statement is honest, but it is unusually long and specific. A reviewer may read "the prior-art check that identified Bińkowski et al.'s Appendix B.2 as the source of the mixture identity ... and the limitation already stated by Merino et al." as saying the LLM found the paper's key prior art and its motivating quote. "Used substantially" also invites scrutiny. Suggested rewrite (3-4 lines): "A generative AI assistant was used for editing text, implementing figure code and parts of the evaluation suite, and literature search. Research questions, experimental design and interpretation are the authors'. No numerical result was produced by a language model; every number is emitted by released code from a logged run." Keep the "every numeral is re-derived by a registered check" claim only if the released code really does this, because a reviewer may check. In the supp: remove the TODO. Be ready for an ethics flag on "a GrabCraft scrape" when GrabCraft's terms restrict distribution (supp l.1150). The mitigation (URLs, hashes and a rebuild script, no redistribution) is good, so put a one-line pointer to it in the main paper §5. The "institutional determination" placeholder must be resolved or removed.

### Other places that over-hedge or overclaim
- **Over-hedged or defensive:** "It measures what the render tier cannot, and not structural quality outright" (l.276-277); "A cutaway is not itself a good metric..." (l.449-451); "Gates need auditing as much as metrics do" (l.323-324); "The property we defend is the maximum rather than the mean" (l.304). Each is fine once, but together they make the paper sound like it is answering reviews before they exist. Fold them into the limitations section.
- **Overclaims:** "Every observer misses the interior" (§6.1 title, Table 2), which should say *exterior* observer. "Across 40 generators" (abstract) without "from one project". "The same audit is owed to mesh and implicit 3D generation" (l.609), untested. "Fréchet distance fails" (abstract) as if new. "Provably" (l.452) for something that is immediate. "Three domains" for an Objaverse result that gets three lines. "No block count could produce that pair of rows" (l.557), which is good rhetoric and is earned, so keep it.
- **Numbers without context:** "+422.43" with two decimals on a noisy z-score (use 422); "4.4 times"; "1,270"; "Spearman 0.96"; "12.08 / 15.06" raw MMD² with no scale; "median 1.23% of pixels at a median maximum channel difference of 128/255" (l.461-462); "10.9×" calibration inflation (l.345, the line the authors' own comment flags, which belongs in supp).
- **Notation:** l.332 "a 323 box" and l.373 "a 203 grid" lost their superscripts in text extraction. Check that they render as 32³ and 20³ (they likely do in the PDF). "MMD2" in Table 4 should be MMD².
- **Section 2, first paragraph** (l.113-117) is one sentence that adds nothing. Cut it or merge it.
- **§3 noise-floor definition** (l.213-221) is good, but it should come with the one-sentence rule "|z|<2 = indistinguishable from real", which the figures rely on.

## 3. Highest-leverage changes before Nov 16 (~7 days), ranked

### Rewrite / reframe (do all of these; together about 2.5 days)
**R1 (1 day, highest leverage). Reframe around "this changes which model wins, and here is the fix", not "renders can't see inside".** Rewrite the abstract, intro and contributions as in §2 items 1-5. Lead with the GROW-SHELL control and the rank reversals, and treat the theorem as a one-line motivation. Add the reversed-pair figure (Supp Fig. 11 with the roof-off view) to the main paper, and make room by cutting repetition in §6.1-6.2 and shrinking Table 2's caption. State "40 generators from one project" in the main paper yourself; it is worse if a reviewer finds it in supp.

**R2 (0.5-1 day). Consistency and cleanup pass.** Remove all Comment/TODO text. Reconcile Fig. 2 (0.89 vs 3.9; 56.5 vs 55.8; the "6.7" lower bound). Choose one canonical FILL-INTERIOR MV-KID number per claim and label the provenance of the others. Use one name for the unit, one for "pillar/tier", and Table 1/Table 4 category labels that agree. Name the five pillars. Define "spreads" or remove it. Shorten the LLM statement and check the page limit. Add a Makefile check for "Comment:" and "TODO".

**R3 (0.5 day). Restructure Table 4 and §6.4.** Split ranked submissions from probes and DQs, add run-in heads in §6.4, and delete the meaningless ratios (1,270×, 4.4×).

### New experiments (pick one, or two if there is time)
**E1 (1.5-2 days, the best answer to W2). Show which ranking is right on the 76 reversed pairs.** Pick roughly 30-40 of the reversed generator pairs. Score each with an *interior-aware* judge that is independent of V-KID: (a) the GPT-4o pairwise judge on roof-off or cutaway views, which you already have pre-registered and which works at 0.70 on probes; and/or (b) a functional criterion, such as the fraction of builds a simulated player can enter or walk through, or the fraction with at least one room. Report "on reversed pairs, the interior-aware judge sides with V-KID in X% (CI)". A small human 2AFC on cutaways (about 2 raters × 40 pairs, roughly 2 hours of rater time) would be stronger still. This one number turns W2 from "no validation" into "validated against an independent interior-aware observer", and it answers W1 because it shows the blindness *matters*.

**E2 (2-3 days, answers W3 and the "why should mesh people care" question). One more external generator.** Options: run one off-the-shelf text- or image-to-3D model (e.g. TRELLIS or Hunyuan3D) on about 256 "house" prompts, voxelize at 32³, and score it under MV-KID vs V-KID. Expect shells, so HOLLOW-SHELL-like behaviour: render-close, voxel-far. Or run a second LEGO generator next to BrickGPT. This would back the conclusion's "owed to mesh and implicit generation" with a measurement, and it would give the reversal claim a generator that is not in-house. Riskier on time. Do this only if E1 finishes by day 4.

**E3 (0.5 day, cheap insurance for W4).** Move the random-3D-CNN / generic-descriptor result (Supp J) into the main text as one sentence plus a number, and if feasible add one *learned* volumetric feature, such as a small 3D CNN trained self-supervised on the training split, as a V-KID alternative that passes the gates. This shows the protocol, not the 77-d design, is the contribution. It is low effort if the Supp J numbers already exist, so it is mostly rewriting.

**Suggested schedule:** Day 1-2: R2 + R1 drafting, with E1 running in parallel (GPT-4o roof-off judge on reversed pairs, plus a functional check). Day 3-4: E1 results into the abstract and intro; R3; new main figure. Day 5: E2 if feasible, otherwise E3. Day 6: full read-through by someone outside the project for jargon. Day 7: buffer, supp TODOs, anonymized code URL, page-limit check.
