% \begin{abstract}
% Many existing evaluation protocols for 3D generative models assess realism by rendering generated structures from a limited set of viewpoints and comparing pretrained visual features of these renderings against those of real data. While effective for measuring visual similarity and distributional realism, such protocols provide limited guarantees about properties that are intrinsically three-dimensional or functional. In particular, exterior-view evaluation may fail to detect missing or incoherent interior geometry, inaccessible spaces, collisions, or invalid spatial layouts. For structures intended to be inhabited, assembled, or navigated—such as Minecraft buildings, LEGO models, or game environments—these properties are central to usability rather than merely appearance.   We measure this failure with a controlled corruption. Filling every sealed void in a corpus of real buildings adds a median of $33\%$ more blocks and leaves the exterior silhouette untouched. A multi-view DINOv2 kernel distance places the corrupted corpus at just $0.38$ noise floors from real data, while our proposed geometric metric on voxel descriptors (V-KID) successfully separates it by over $200$ noise floors. Seven render-space statistics, four encoders, view budgets up to sixteen views, and a second domain all miss this corruption while resolving every other one we apply. Under an opaque renderer, filling sealed voids cannot change any exterior view, meaning no exterior-view statistic can separate it at any sample size.   To close this blind spot, we introduce a validation protocol that requires metrics to pass strict gates for monotonicity, invariance, and sample-size stability before they can rank models. Measuring geometry directly alongside renders provides a complete evaluation. Applying this protocol to our own models demonstrated its practical necessity by exposing an $87\%$ train-test contamination in a generator we had treated as a top result, proving the danger of deploying unvalidated metrics.
% \end{abstract}
